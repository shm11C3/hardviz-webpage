## Where the other tools do more

- **Voltages and motherboard coverage.** HWiNFO, HWMonitor, and LibreHardwareMonitor read voltages, which HardwareVisualizer does not. HardwareVisualizer reads motherboard temperatures and fans only on the chips listed in the [compatibility notes](/specs/).
- **In-game overlay.** MSI Afterburner, with RivaTuner Statistics Server, and HWiNFO can show readings on screen while a game runs. HardwareVisualizer has no overlay.
- **Fan control and overclocking.** MSI Afterburner adjusts GPU clocks, voltage, and fan curves. HardwareVisualizer reads sensors and does not change hardware settings.
- **Log files.** HWiNFO, HWMonitor, and LibreHardwareMonitor can write readings to CSV files that you can open in other software. HardwareVisualizer keeps history in its own database and has no export.
- **Nothing to install.** Task Manager is already part of Windows.

## What HardwareVisualizer is built around

- **History you can read inside the app.** Recording is on by default. Insights charts periods from 10 minutes to 30 days, Cooling Insight from 24 hours to 1 year, and history is kept for 365 days by default.
- **Temperature compared at the same load.** [Cooling Insight](/cooling-insight/) compares CPU temperature with your PC's own baseline within each CPU-load band, and can take room temperature into account.
- **One application on three operating systems.** Windows, macOS, and Linux, with [different sensor coverage on each](/#platforms).
- **Open source, with signed Windows and macOS builds.** See [Security and data](/security/).
